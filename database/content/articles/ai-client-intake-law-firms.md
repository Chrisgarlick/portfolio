Client intake is one of the most repetitive jobs in a law firm, and one of the most expensive to get wrong. A slow reply loses the client to the firm that answered first. A missed conflict check is worse. And the work usually falls to someone whose time is billed at far more than data entry is worth.

## What intake looks like today

In most firms, getting from an enquiry to an open matter takes around eight separate steps: acknowledging the enquiry, gathering the details, running a conflict check, collecting ID, preparing the engagement letter, chasing the signature, setting up the matter, and filing it all in the practice management system. Each step is simple. Together they take hours a week, and the details get typed in two or three times along the way.

## What gets automated

An AI-assisted intake handles the reading, the retyping and the boilerplate, and leaves the judgement to people:

- **Acknowledgement:** every enquiry gets a prompt, relevant reply, at any hour
- **Details captured once:** the information from the enquiry form or email is structured and reused, not retyped
- **Conflict check prepared:** names and parties are checked against your records and anything uncertain is flagged for a person
- **ID collection:** requests and reminders sent automatically
- **Engagement letter drafted** from your template and the details already gathered
- **Matter set up** in your practice management system once it is approved

Eight steps become two or three moments where a fee-earner reviews and approves.

## AI drafts, people approve

The rule for any system that touches a matter file is simple: the AI prepares, a person decides. It never sends a substantive email, files a document or makes a legal judgement on its own. What it removes is the reading, the data entry and the drafting, not the lawyer.

Everything is logged, the system only reaches the data it needs, and the data stays in the systems you already use, whether that is Clio, Leap, iManage, NetDocuments or Microsoft 365. That makes the conversations about the SRA, GDPR and the ICO much easier.

## Is it right for your firm?

It is usually a good fit if intake happens at least ten times a month, if fee-earners are visibly spending time on it, and if you are comfortable with drafts that a person approves before they go out. For a sole practitioner the volume rarely justifies a build; a well-designed form and a few templates may do the job.
