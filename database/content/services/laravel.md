Most web applications get built once and patched for years. Every patch makes the next one harder, until a small change takes a week and nobody wants to touch the code.

That's not a Laravel problem. It's a build problem. I build the other way round: tested from the start, structured so it can change, and fast because it was designed to be, not because someone bolted caching on afterwards.

## What I build

- **Web applications** shaped around how your business actually works
- **Admin panels and dashboards** your team will want to use
- **APIs and integrations** with payments, CRMs, email and anything else that has an API
- **Content management** built for your content, not the other way round
- **Rebuilds and upgrades** of applications that have grown slow, fragile or out of date

## How I work

It starts with a proper look at what is there: the code, the hosting, and how the public side performs. Then a written scope. The build lands in small releases you can try as they go, every one covered by automated tests. At the end you get the code, the documentation and the accounts. Nothing is locked to me.

## Proof, not promises

This site runs on [cg-cms](/work/cg-cms), a Laravel CMS I built from scratch. It renders each page once, when it is saved, and serves it straight from disk. A cached page comes back in 4.4 milliseconds, on a server with 1GB of memory. Moving [this site](/work/chrisgarlick-com) onto it kept every one of its 79 URLs working.
