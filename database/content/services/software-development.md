The interesting part of software is rarely the feature list. It's understanding the process well enough that the software makes it simpler, instead of adding one more place to click.

## What I build

- **Internal tools** for the jobs your team currently does in spreadsheets and email
- **Integrations** that get your systems talking, so nobody copies data from one screen to another
- **Services and APIs** that other software relies on, such as document rendering or data processing
- **Automation** for the scheduled, repetitive work that keeps a business running

## Who it suits

It suits a business that runs on a process no off-the-shelf product quite fits. Often that means spreadsheets passed around by email, or the same data typed into two systems. If your team has built workarounds to get through the week, there is usually a tool worth building.

It suits less well if an existing product already does the job. If one does, I'll say so. Buying is often the better choice.

## How I work

Understand the process first, write the scope down, build it with tests, and hand it over documented. Most of what I build is in [Laravel](/services/laravel) and PHP, with TypeScript and Node.js where they fit better.

In practice, the first call is about the process, not the software. I want to know who does what, where it slows down and what goes wrong. Then I write the scope: what the tool does, what done looks like, and roughly how long it will take.

The build lands in small releases you can try as they go. Each one is covered by automated tests, so later changes don't break what already works. You talk to me throughout, and I'm the one writing the code. If a choice needs your input, I ask you directly.

At handover you get the code, the documentation and the accounts. Nothing is locked to me.

## After launch

Software a business runs on keeps changing, because the business does. Tests make that safe. I can stay on for ongoing support, or hand it to your own team. What I don't offer is a 24/7 support desk. If you need one, an agency is the better fit.

## Proof

[cg-cms](/work/cg-cms) is a good example. It is a whole CMS packaged as a Laravel library, with generators for new content types. A command line lets tools like Claude Code draft content under the same rules as the editor.

## Common questions

### Can you connect it to the systems we already use?

Yes. Integrations are a big part of this work. I connect to what your team already uses, such as Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Xero, HubSpot and Notion. It's direct API work, not no-code glue.

### Do I own the code?

Yes. You get the code, the documentation and the accounts at handover. Nothing is locked to me, so another developer could pick it up and carry on.

### Why Laravel?

It's what I know best, and it suits business systems well: data, users, queues and integrations. Where TypeScript and Node.js fit the job better, I use those instead.

### What if the scope changes during the build?

It often does, once people start using the first release. Because the work lands in small releases, we can adjust the next one rather than unpick a finished build. Any change gets written into the scope, so we both still know what done looks like.
